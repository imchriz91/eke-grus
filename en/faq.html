FAQ

Which material suits you?

We get asked about material choices all the time. Here’s our general advice — always get in touch so we can help you choose based on what we have in stock right now.

What material is best for a driveway?

A durable driveway is usually built in two layers: a base layer of coarser crushed stone that compacts into a stable foundation, and a thinner top layer of finer crushed material laid over it for a smooth surface to drive and walk on.

Crushed material is preferable to round natural gravel here — the angular pieces lock together and compact, while rounded stones tend to shift and roll under vehicle weight.

What should I use for drainage?

For drainage you want a coarser, clean material with as few fines (stone dust and silt) as possible, so water can pass through freely without clogging. It’s commonly used around drainage pipes, foundations, and soakaway trenches.

Tell us about the project when you call — the right grain size depends on how much water needs to move and where in the structure the material sits.

What’s best for garden beds and borders?

For planting itself, roots need a well-balanced, well-draining soil to grow in — not a straight aggregate. For paths and areas between plantings, many people choose a finer decorative gravel or pebble that also helps keep weeds down.

Ask us about current soil and gravel options — the range shifts a bit with the season.

How do I choose the right gravel or stone size?

As a rule of thumb: finer fractions (roughly 0–8 mm) work well for paths and top-dressing, mid-size fractions (roughly 8–16 mm) are versatile and suit most driveways and garden projects, and coarser fractions (16 mm and up) go into base layers and drainage where water flow and load matter most.

Exact sizes and names vary between quarries — check our price list for what we currently have, or call and we’ll guide you.

How much material do I need to order?

Work out volume by multiplying area by depth: length × width × depth = cubic metres (m³). As a guide, a base layer is often around 10–15 cm deep with a thinner 3–5 cm top layer, though this varies with the ground and intended use.

Order a bit extra, around 10%, to cover compaction and waste. Not sure about the math? Call us with your measurements and we’ll help you estimate.

Should I lay landscape fabric under the gravel?

In most cases, yes. A landscape fabric (geotextile) between the soil and the base layer stops weeds pushing up and keeps the gravel from sinking into the soil beneath over time, which keeps the surface looking neat and staying stable for longer — especially important under paths and driveways.

What’s the difference between crushed rock and natural gravel?

Crushed rock has angular, irregular grains that lock together and compact into a stable, load-bearing surface — that’s why it’s the first choice for base layers, driveways and other areas under load.

Natural gravel has rounder grains that sit more loosely. It’s often chosen as a decorative top layer or where water permeability matters more than load-bearing strength, but it compacts less well under weight.

Can I collect it myself, or do you deliver?

Both work well. Bring a trailer and pick up at the pit yourself — payment is taken on site by card or cash, and if you need help loading material onto your trailer, a small fee applies. Want it delivered instead? We can arrange transport — contact us for a quote.

How do I order, and what does it cost?

The easiest way is to call or email us — see our price list for a sense of what we typically carry and roughly what it costs. Since stock and prices vary with the season, we always confirm the current price and availability with you directly before you collect or we deliver.
